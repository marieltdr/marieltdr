UC Berkeley Research: This Place is Our Archive

A research project within the Racial and Anti-Colonial Ecologies Lab of UC Berkeley, working to preserve the historically Black environmental justice community of Port Arthur, Texas, by mapping its past and present. The site of the U.S.A's largest oil refinery, along with many more refineries, Port Arthur has experienced environmental contamination and the destruction of historic sites. This, combined with repeated severe storm events, has created the need for archival work and extraction of contamination history within the trees in the area.

Potential New MUNI Station Locations in San Francisco, CA

An interactive storymap exploring ideal MUNI (bus) station locations in San Francisco using a multi-criteria suitability analysis. This project addresses the problem of transportation accessibility in SF, specifically for low-income communities of color with low car ownership, by locating the three most ideal potential sites for a new MUNI Station: the Sunset District, Excelsior District, and Portola neighborhood.

Native American Health Equity in Riverside County, CA

In Riverside County, Native American residents experience worse health outcomes than other populations, according to the Riverside University Health System and Strategic Health Alliance Pursuing Equity (SHAPE) of Riverside County. Using Census Bureau and county open data, this project precisely visualizes such health disparities through four thematic maps.

This Place is Our Archive

Session: Hazards, Risks, & Disasters | San Francisco, CA

We worked with residents in the West Side of Port Arthur to preserve and map historically Black locations that now primarily exist in their memories after decades of refinery expansion, environmental contamination, and major storm events have destroyed their community. Using a combination of Port Arthur oral histories, Google MyMaps, ArcGIS StoryMaps, and video editing software, we created an audio-visual depiction of how Black relationships to place and environment developed in the West Side, and how the oil refineries and climate change complicate them. Overall, this project aims to preserve the longstanding Black relationships to the places built and destroyed, but not forgotten, in the West Side of Port Arthur.

Food Insecurity, Urban Farming, and Community Empowerment

Question: How do community farms in California draw on broader systemic and historical inequalities and resistance to those inequalities to advance food sovereignty, social justice, and community empowerment?

Recently, California has seen a massive increase in rates of food insecurity, with 22% of all households suffering from it. Of these households facing food insecurity, a disproportionate number are Black and Latine. However, food insecurity is not new: it has a complex history within numerous communities, especially communities of color. In response to food insecurity, these communities of color have come together to form community farms that don’t just combat food insecurity but also advocate for food sovereignty and food justice in the form of education, cooperation, and empowerment. This StoryMap aims to explore the link between the history of food insecurity within communities of color in California and our present fight for a future of food sovereignty, in which empowerment and sustainability are at the forefront.
