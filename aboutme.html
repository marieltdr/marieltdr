Background

Hello! My name is Mariel Del Rosario, and I recently graduated from the University of California, Berkeley, with a Bachelor of Arts in Geography. I achieved Highest Honors in my degree, as well as graduating With Distinction (equivalent to cum laude) for my overall academic performance. My coursework primarily consisted of courses in Geospatial Representation, especially Geographic Information Systems. I also took additional coursework in Data Science, focusing on building programming skills (Python, R, SQL, Pandas, Jupyter/Colab) to further my breadth of abilities in GIS. With this degree and skills, I aspire towards a career in Geospatial Data Science.

My journey towards GIS and Data Science began with my hometown of Palm Springs, CA. I grew up desiring to understand and solve the geographical challenges of the harsh desert environment that Palm Springs lies in -- urban heat islands, air quality issues, and flash flood events. With a career in Geospatial Data Science, I aim to help communities feel more prepared for catastrophic events, as well as tackle the infrastructure issues that plague many cities today.

During my time at UC Berkeley, I became a Research Assistant for Dr. Tianna Bruno in the Geography Department for the project Spaces Where There Were Places, which focuses on preserving and mapping the history of the historically Black neighborhood of the West Side of Port Arthur, TX. In this project, I started by working in a team of three to create a database of historic Black businesses, churches, schools, and homes mentioned in interviews conducted in Port Arthur by the project leader, Mr. Syers. By conducting archival research and comparing & transcribing interviews, we were able to locate the majority of places, which I then visualized into a Google MyMap. For the remainder of the project, I created an ArcGIS StoryMap (This Place is Our Archive) introducing the project and visualizing the West Side of Port Arthur through images, streetview, and mapping. Most notably, my work on this project led me to present our work at the Association of American Geographers Conference in March 2026.

Currently, I'm looking for entry-level roles and internships focused on GIS, data analytics, and/or urban planning.
